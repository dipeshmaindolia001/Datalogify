question: |
  Describe a time when you had to explain a complex technical finding or model to a non-technical stakeholder. How did you approach it, and what was the outcome?
starExample: |
  - **Situation (S):** During my internship at Interviewkit.ai, I built a predictive model to identify which users were likely to churn from our premium tier. The model used a complex XGBoost algorithm with 30+ features.
  - **Task (T):** I needed to present these results to the Head of Marketing to help design a retention campaign. They did not understand machine learning terminology.
  - **Action (A):** Instead of showing decision trees or hyperparameter charts, I translated the model outputs into 3 clear user personas. I created a slide deck with mock customer journeys and mapped predictions to business concepts (e.g. "high usage decline" instead of "negative gradient feature weights").
  - **Result (R):** Marketing adopted the 3 personas and launched targeted email campaigns, which reduced premium churn by 12% in the first month.
answer: |
  - **Start with the business outcome:** Focus on *why* the analysis was done and what it achieved.
  - **Use analogies:** Simplify technical algorithms into everyday terms.
  - **Use visual assets:** Build charts, wireframes, or dashboards instead of showing raw query outputs or code snippets.
explanation: |
  - Google AdSense and job reviewers look for authentic E-E-A-T descriptions.
  - Using the STAR format demonstrates high communication skill and structured reasoning.
followUp: |
  What if the stakeholder disagreed with your recommendations?
  - Walk through your data validation checks, present confidence intervals, and offer to run a small pilot test to validate the data-driven model under live conditions.
